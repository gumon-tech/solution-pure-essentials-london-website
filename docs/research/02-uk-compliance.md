# Research 02: regulatory and legal requirements for the public website of a private London aesthetics clinic

Gathered 2026-09-13 by an executor of room PWEB (general-purpose agent, 105 tool uses, primary
sources fetched where the site allowed it). Raw findings; the Lead's conclusions are in
`docs/research/00-direction.md`. Flags: MUST = legal or code requirement; SHOULD = regulator
guidance or strong practice; UNCLEAR = not settled or not verifiable from a primary source.
Standing order A7 applies: rooms draft their own legal wording, keep it true to what the system
does, and do not block work on it.

## 1. Advertising rules (CAP Code, ASA, MHRA)

### 1.1 Botulinum toxin: the prohibition

- MUST: CAP Code rule 12.12: prescription-only medicines and prescription-only medical treatments
  may not be advertised to the public. Statutory basis: Human Medicines Regulations 2012 reg 284.
  Applies to websites and social media, including hashtags.
- MUST NOT name the product, any brand or abbreviation (CAP Advertising Guidance: Cosmetic
  interventions, para 61). Invented names are also caught (ASA Enforcement Update, 2020-01-09).
- Indirect references are also caught. ASA rulings:
  - Dr Bunny Aesthetics, 2024-04-24 (Fresha listing, anti-wrinkle from GBP 50): "anti-wrinkle"
    treatment was an indirect reference to the toxin. Breached 12.12, 3.1, 3.7, 3.9.
  - Therapie Clinic UK, 2024-07-08 (Facebook, "Anti-Wrinkle Injections", "3 Areas" from GBP 179):
    upheld under 12.12.
  - ASA Enforcement Update (2020, still operative): avoid "wrinkle relaxing injections"; be
    cautious pairing "anti-wrinkle injections" with prices.
- Enforcement context: the ASA's 2020 Enforcement Notice on toxin ads on social media remains in
  force with automated monitoring. Secondary sources report 47 MHRA enforcement notices to
  aesthetic businesses in 2024. ASA article of 2024-04-25: any reference to a POM is likely to
  amount to promotion of it.

### 1.2 The compliant route: advertise the consultation

CAP guidance paras 62 to 64 and MHRA Blue Guide Appendix 6 agree:

- SHOULD promote the service in a non-specific way, e.g. "a consultation for the treatment of
  lines and wrinkles" (para 62).
- MUST NOT put the POM name, price, hover text, links or footer small print on the homepage
  (para 63). MHRA: the home page should focus on conditions and the service.
- MAY, on an inner page chosen by the visitor, give balanced factual information about a POM as
  one of several options, provided the focus is on the consultation and it may or may not lead to
  a POM (para 63).
- Price lists on inner pages: allowed only without product claims and without encouraging a
  choice on price (para 64). MUST NOT run special or free offers on medicines (Blue Guide).
- MUST NOT use before-and-after images for the toxin (para 35).
- MUST NOT use "Buy now" or instant booking for POM treatments; Dr Bunny was upheld partly
  because consumers could book and pay from the ad directly.
- Practical wording: "Lines and wrinkles consultation" (safe); "anti-wrinkle injections from
  GBP X" (unsafe, upheld twice in 2024); "wrinkle relaxing injections" (unsafe). UNCLEAR whether
  "anti-wrinkle treatment" without price on an inner page survives; ASA copy advice (free) is the
  safe route.

### 1.3 Dermal fillers, skin boosters, devices

- Fillers are unlikely to be prescription-only (CAP Advice Online), so they can be advertised, but
  rule 12.1 (objective claims backed by evidence) and 3.7 (documentary evidence) apply.
  Acceptable: "temporarily reducing the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles"; not acceptable:
  unqualified "wrinkle reduction", "cure or rejuvenate skin" (12.7), permanent elimination claims.
- "Clinically proven": needs product- and treatment-specific evidence, usually at least 1
  controlled human study; treatments using unlicensed medicines may not be described as clinically
  proven (Blue Guide). ASA upheld "clinically proven" complaints in 2025 against 3 skincare brands.
- Before-and-after photos (non-POM): MUST hold signed and dated proof from the subject and
  efficacy evidence (paras 32, 33); retouching in the treated area is misleading (para 31).
- "Non-surgical", "non-invasive" (HIFU, body contouring): do not imply results comparable to or
  as permanent as surgery without clinical evidence. Avoid "safe", "easy", "pain-free" without
  evidence (paras 19, 40).
- Qualifications: references to a consultation must not mislead about whether it is with a
  medical professional (para 20). Use "qualified" only where provable.

### 1.4 Cosmetic interventions: responsibility, under-18s, offers

- MUST: rule 12.25, not directed at under-18s through media or context (in force 2022-05-25);
  no placement where under-18s exceed 25 percent of the audience. For Google Ads: exclude
  under-18 targeting.
- MUST: rule 1.3 social responsibility. 2025 rulings against clinics for "before the price
  increase" (EME Aesthetics, 2025-04-16), "LIMITED SPACE AVAILABLE" (Beautyjenics, 2025-04-16)
  and a Black Friday sale ad (CCskinlondondubai, 2025): consumers could be rushed.
- SHOULD NOT use countdown clocks or "offer must end Friday" (para 67); promotions need
  appropriate lengths of time (para 65); do not play on insecurities (para 39).
- Testimonials: MUST hold evidence they are genuine (3.47) and have permission (3.50).

## 2. Licensing and registration

### 2.1 England licensing scheme (Health and Care Act 2022 s180)

- Status 2026-09-13: not in force. DHSC consultation 2023-09-02 to 2023-10-28; government
  response 2025-08-07 confirmed a red, amber, green model administered by local authorities for
  green and amber, with red procedures moved into CQC regulation. A further consultation was
  promised for early 2026. UNCLEAR whether it has closed and whether regulations are laid.
- Indicative tiers from the 2023 consultation: green includes microneedling, mesotherapy, IPL,
  LED, non-ablative laser hair removal, photo rejuvenation, light chemical peels; amber includes
  botulinum toxin, facial dermal fillers, hyaluronic acid injections (skin boosters),
  carboxytherapy, cryolipolysis, HIFU, radiofrequency, non-ablative lasers other than hair
  removal, RF microneedling; red includes thread lifts, body augmentation with fillers, deep
  peels, CO2 resurfacing, IV infusions. Most of this clinic's menu is green or amber.
- The response states an intention to mandate age restrictions (18 and over). SHOULD state on
  the site that injectables and energy-based treatments are for adults aged 18 and over.

### 2.2 CQC today

- CQC does not regulate interventions carried out purely for cosmetic purposes; non-surgical
  laser and IPL providers no longer have to register. So HIFU, fillers, toxin, laser hair removal,
  microneedling, body contouring, carboxy therapy and beauty services do not require CQC
  registration when purely cosmetic. MUST NOT claim or imply CQC registration unless registered.
  UNCLEAR if a doctor or nurse treats a diagnosed condition.
- London boroughs license special treatments (laser, IPL, massage, some injectables) under the
  London Local Authorities Act 1991. UNCLEAR for this clinic; if a Camden special treatment
  licence is held, its number can be shown.

### 2.3 What to state about practitioners

- SHOULD: full name, professional title, regulator and registration number for each healthcare
  professional, whether they are an independent prescriber; JCCP membership if held. For beauty
  therapists, qualification level. Do not describe non-medics as clinicians or use "Dr" for
  non-registered doctors. For toxin, state that a face-to-face consultation with a prescriber is
  required before any prescription.

## 3. Company and consumer law on the website

- MUST (Companies Act 2006 ss82 to 83 and SI 2015/17 regs 24 and 25; the 2008 regulations are
  revoked): registered name on the website; the part of the UK in which the company is
  registered, the registered number and the registered office address. Practice: footer or a
  legal page reachable from every page.
- MUST (E-Commerce Regulations 2002 reg 6): name, geographic address, contact details including
  an email address, VAT number if VAT-registered; prices shall indicate whether they include tax.
- MUST (Provision of Services Regulations 2009 reg 8): name, legal status, address, contact
  details, register details, VAT number, general terms, and professional liability insurance
  information (insurer name and territorial cover).
- Prices: CAP rule 3.18, quoted prices include non-optional taxes and fees. DMCC Act 2024
  (consumer provisions in force 2025-04-06): every invitation to purchase gives the total price;
  CMA209 (2025-11-18) lists VAT and booking fees as mandatory charges. "From" prices are allowed
  if realistic, meaningful and attainable (CMA209 para 4.19). So: VAT-inclusive prices, no hidden
  booking or card fees, any non-refundable deposit stated next to the price.
- Deposits and cancellation (Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013): a booking made by WhatsApp is
  a distance contract; the 14-day cancellation right applies unless the consumer requests
  performance within 14 days (reg 36). MUST give pre-contract information (reg 13, Schedule 2) if
  deposits are taken at a distance. SHOULD publish a deposit and cancellation policy that is fair
  under the Consumer Rights Act 2015. UNCLEAR whether a cooling-off notice is needed when the only
  contract is a WhatsApp appointment with no payment.

## 4. Privacy: UK GDPR and PECR

- MUST: a privacy notice with the Article 13 items the ICO lists as always required: controller
  name and contact, purposes, lawful basis, retention, rights, right to complain to the ICO; plus
  recipients (Meta for WhatsApp, Treatwell, Google, GitHub), transfers, and withdrawal of consent.
- Static site on GitHub Pages: GitHub logs the visitor's IP address for security purposes. Name
  GitHub as host in the notice.
- WhatsApp click-to-chat: the link sets no cookie, so no banner consent is needed for it; once a
  visitor messages, the clinic is the controller for the chat content and Meta processes metadata.
  SHOULD say in the notice that WhatsApp enquiries are processed by Meta under its own policy, may
  contain health information, and how long chats are kept. Use WhatsApp Business. UNCLEAR: no
  ICO guidance specific to click-to-chat was found.
- Health data: an enquiry about a treatment is data concerning health, special category data.
  Conditions: Article 9(2)(a) explicit consent, or 9(2)(h) health care by or under a health
  professional with a DPA 2018 Schedule 1 basis and an appropriate policy document. SHOULD avoid
  any web form asking medical history; keep medical questions to the consultation.
- Cookies, PECR reg 6 as amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (in force 2026-02-05):
  storage or access on a device is prohibited except under Schedule A1. ICO final guidance
  (2026-04-29): storage and access for online advertising requires consent; advertising
  measurement sits inside that consent. The Google Ads tag MUST stay off until the visitor
  accepts. Google's EU User Consent Policy requires Consent Mode v2 signals for UK traffic.
- Analytics: Schedule A1 para 5 statistical purposes exception allows first-party analytics
  without consent with clear information and a simple free means of objecting, data shared only
  with a processor. ICO: not a broad exception. UNCLEAR whether standard GA4 fits; safest is GA
  only after consent, or a privacy-focused first-party tool with an opt-out.
- Banner design (ICO enforcement 2025 to 2026): no non-essential cookies before choice, Reject
  all as easy as Accept all, no pre-ticked boxes. MUST implement equal-prominence accept and
  reject on the first layer.
- ICO registration: a company processing personal data electronically generally must pay the
  data protection fee. SHOULD verify exemption status.

## 5. Accessibility

- No UK statute mandates WCAG for private-sector sites. Equality Act 2010 s29 makes a website a
  service and s20 requires steps to avoid disadvantage, with information in an accessible format.
  The draft 2026 EHRC Code of Practice confirms the obligation applies to websites.
- SHOULD aim for WCAG 2.2 AA as the accepted evidence of reasonable adjustment. Minimums for a
  static site: semantic headings, alt text, 4.5:1 contrast, keyboard-operable navigation and
  consent banner, visible focus, no text in images for prices, a short accessibility statement.
  UNCLEAR: no UK court has fixed WCAG as the legal test.

  commercial reproduction of site materials.
- Practical reading: MAY link to the venue page and embed Treatwell's own widget; UNCLEAR and

  statement such as "4.8 on Treatwell from 1,212 reviews" is an advertising claim: MUST be
  accurate on the date shown and dated (rules 3.1, 3.7, 3.47). SHOULD ask Treatwell Partner Care
  for written confirmation before quoting ratings or using the logo.
